How to read these scripts: each `##` heading is one scene. The `> ` line says what is on
screen; the text under it is the voiceover, which also becomes the captions.

## problem
> title: Your architecture lives in a hundred places | spreadsheets · diagrams · slide decks · people's heads

Ask five people how your systems fit together and you get five spreadsheets, three diagrams
and a slide deck from last year. None of them agree, and none of them are current.

## idea
> diagram: one-map

Atrium gives your organisation one living map. Capabilities, applications, data and
technology, and how they connect. Architects keep it right. Managers use it to decide.
Engineers, and their AI coding assistants, build with it.

## first-run
> shot: first-run

Getting started takes a minute. Load a sample, or drop in the spreadsheet you already have.
Atrium recognises the columns, fills in what it can work out, and draws your first
capability map.

## explore
> shot: explore-map

Click anything to see what it supports, what it depends on and who owns it. Colour the map by
health or cost, and switch between today and the target state you are working towards.

## decide
> shot: dashboard

Managers get answers without the archaeology. What it costs, what is at risk, what reaches end
of life, and where the money goes. One click turns a dashboard into an editable presentation.

## build
> shot: agent-connect

Engineers connect their coding assistant with one command. It reads the architecture and the
standards while it writes code, and suggests updates for an architect to approve.

## close
> end: One shared map, kept current by the people and tools that change it.

One shared map, kept current by the people and the tools that change it. That's Atrium.
